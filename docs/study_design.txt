Core Tier-2 Feature Fidelity Assessment for Synthetic
Histopathology Images
1. Scientific Rationale
Synthetic histopathology images are increasingly used for augmentation, privacy-preserving
sharing, and AI training. However, perceptual realism (Tier-1) does not guarantee preservation
of diagnostically relevant features (Tier-2). This document specifies (i) a four-center, blinded
reader study to validate a concise Tier-2 protocol and (ii) a Tier-3 algorithmic evaluation as-
sessing downstream utility using multiple foundation-model feature extractors.
2. Research Questions and Hypotheses
2.1 Primary Research Question (Multi-center reader study)
Is the Core Tier-2 scoring protocol reproducible across institutions and does it reliably distin-
guish real from synthetic patches based on feature fidelity?
2.2 Primary Hypothesis
H1: Inter-rater reliability of the Core Tier-2 total score is high across centers.
Target: ICC ≥ 0.75
2.3 Secondary Hypotheses
• H2: Center effects are small relative to between-image variability.
• H3: Synthetic patches have lower Tier-2 scores than real patches.
• H4: Key failure modes (e.g., stroma/ECM, TIL plausibility, synthetic artifacts) are consis-
tently detected across centers.
2.4 Tier-3 Research Question (algorithmic utility)
Does training with synthetic augmentation improve downstream performance and generalization
to external centers across multiple foundation-model feature pipelines?
2.5 Tier-3 Hypotheses
• H5: Synthetic augmentation improves out-of-center performance (Centers B–D) relative to
real-only training.
• H6: Improvements are consistent across multiple foundation-model feature extractors (ro-
bustness to representation choice).

3. Study Overview
• Tier-2 (Human): Four-center reader study (4 centers × 2 readers = 8 readers), blinded,
using a centrally curated patch set (N = 180).
• Tier-3 (Algorithmic): Single-center training (Center A) with external testing on Centers
B–D, repeated across 4 foundation-model feature extractors.

4.3 The Concrete Question(s) Asked to Raters
Session-level instruction (shown once) Instruction: You will evaluate histopathology
patches. Please assess feature fidelity and histologic consistency at the level of diagnosti-
cally relevant structures. Do not attempt to decide whether a patch is real or synthetic.
Per-patch umbrella question Umbrella question: “Are the diagnostically relevant fea-
tures in this patch consistent with real H&E histology at this magnification, without non-physiologic
patterns?”
Per-patch structured questions (Core Tier-2, 6 items) Each item is scored on a 3-point
ordinal scale:
0 = inconsistent / clearly non-physiologic, 1 = borderline / minor inconsistencies, 2 = consistent with real
1. F1 Nuclear detail & cytology: Are nuclear size/shape, chromatin texture, and nucleoli
patterns consistent with real histology (no impossible nuclear patterns)?
2. F2 Tissue architecture: Is the tissue organization (tumor/stroma boundaries, gland/nest
structure, transitions) coherent (no broken topology or impossible transitions)?
3. F3 Stroma / ECM realism: Is stromal/ECM texture consistent (no “plastic” appearance,
melted background, or repeated fibrous motifs)?
4. F4 Inflammation / TIL plausibility: Are inflammatory cells/TIL morphology and spatial
distribution consistent (no “stamped” lymphocytes or unnatural clustering)?
5. F5 Synthetic artifacts / hallucination markers: Are there visible synthetic artifacts
(repetition, tiling seams, edge artifacts, non-biologic textures)? (0=clear artifacts; 1=possi-
ble/subtle; 2=none visible)
6. F6 Global clinical usability: Would you trust this patch enough to use it as evidence in
diagnostic reasoning? (0=no; 1=limited/uncertain; 2=yes)
Optional single “main failure reason” item (recommended) If any item was scored 0,
select the main reason:
• Nuclear/cytology inconsistency (F1)
• Architecture incoherence (F2)
• Stroma/ECM artifact (F3)
• TIL/inflammation artifact (F4)
• Obvious synthetic artifacts/repetition (F5)
• Other (free text)

4.5 Calibration, Blinding, and Procedure
• Calibration: 15 annotated calibration patches (10–15 minutes).
• Blinding: readers blinded to real/synthetic label, quality band, and source site.
• Procedure: two blocks of 90 patches with recommended break.
• Data capture: reader id, center id, patch id, F1–F6 (0/1/2), optional uncertainty flag, op-
tional timestamps.
4.6 Tier-2 Endpoints
Primary
• ICC for Stotal (two-way random effects, absolute agreement) with 95% CI.
• Fleiss’ κ for Pass/Fail with 95% CI.
Secondary
• Feature-wise agreement (ICC or κ per feature).
• Center effects via mixed-effects modeling.
• Failure-mode consistency across centers (distribution of “main failure reason”).
• Optional intra-rater reliability on repeated items.
4.7 Tier-2 Statistical Model (center effects)
Stotal ∼ center + (1|image) + (1|center:reader)
Report center contrasts (effect size, CI). Conclude robust multi-center reproducibility if center
effects are small relative to between-image variability.
5. Tier-3 Algorithmic Evaluation Plan (Downstream Utility)
5.1 Design Principles
• Training conducted at one center (Center A) to create a strong domain-shift stress test.
• External evaluation on held-out centers (Centers B–D) with no further tuning.
• Repeated across four foundation-model feature extractors to test robustness to repre-
sentation choice.
• Strict leakage controls: synthetic generation uses Center A training split only.
4
5.2 Task Definition
Specify one primary downstream task (choose based on available labels):
• Slide-level classification (preferred): e.g., tumor vs non-tumor; subtype; grade component
proxy.
• Patch-level classification (if only patch labels exist).
All splits must be at patient level (no patient overlap between train/val/test).
5.3 Data Splits
• Center A: train/val split (e.g., 80/20) for model development and early stopping.
• Centers B–D: external test sets (100% held-out), used only once for final reporting.
• No stain/scanner adaptation on B–D beyond fixed preprocessing determined on Center A.
5.4 Training Conditions (per foundation model)
For each representation pipeline, train identical downstream classifiers under:
1. Real-only: train on Center A real data only.
2. Real + Synthetic augmentation: add synthetic samples generated from Center A train
split (stratified by tissue type and quality bands).
3. (Optional) Synthetic-only: stress test; train only on synthetic, evaluate on real.
5.5 Four Foundation-Model Feature Pipelines
Use four distinct, well-known pathology (or general) foundation models as fixed feature extrac-
tors. For each model m ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4}:
• Extract tile/patch embeddings for all WSIs/patches using m.
• Aggregate to slide-level (if needed) using a standard method (e.g., mean pooling, attention
pooling, or MIL head).
• Train a lightweight classifier (e.g., logistic regression / linear layer / shallow MLP) on Center
A train split.
Implementation note: To avoid reviewer debates, keep the downstream head identical
across models wherever possible; the goal is to test robustness to representation choice, not to
optimize architecture.
5.6 Evaluation Metrics
Primary (report per center B, C, D and pooled):
• AUC (or balanced accuracy if binary not appropriate)
• Sensitivity at fixed specificity (clinically interpretable operating point)
• Calibration: Brier score or ECE (Expected Calibration Error)
• 95% CIs via patient-level bootstrap
Secondary:
• Performance vs training-set size (learning curve): n ∈ {10, 20, 50, 100} (if feasible).
• Robustness across tissue strata (tumor/stroma/TIL/mixed).
5
5.7 Leakage and Memorization Controls (must-have with synthetic data)
• Synthetic data must be generated from Center A train split only.
• Nearest-neighbor audit: compute embedding similarity between each synthetic sample and
all real samples in:
– Center A test split (if any),
– Centers B–D test sets.
Flag and remove near-duplicates above a pre-specified similarity threshold.
• Report duplicate rate and confirm no overlap by patient/slide identifiers.
5.8 Tier-3 Primary Endpoint (decision criterion)
Tier-3 “utility” is supported if, for at least 3/4 foundation models, the Real+Synthetic condition
improves external-center performance:
∆AUCext > 0
with consistent direction across B–D (or pooled), and without worsening calibration beyond a
pre-specified tolerance.
6. Risk Mitigation
• Improve reader consistency: calibration + anchored wording (“consistent with real histol-
ogy”).
• Reduce fatigue: two-session design.
• Prevent cherry-picking: stratified random sampling rule fixed a priori.
• Prevent post-hoc tuning: thresholds fixed before analysis.
• Prevent leakage: strict train-only synthesis + nearest-neighbor audits.
7. Ethics and Data Protection
Only de-identified patches/embeddings are used. No patient-identifying information is collected.
Local exemption/waiver determination may be sought if required for reader participation.
